Live UTC positions use approximate J2000 ecliptic ephemerides from JPL orbital elements. Natural satellites use approximate parent-relative mean-element models: the Moon, both Martian moons, Jupiter's eight regular inner moons, Saturn's eight principal moons, Uranus's five large moons, Triton, Proteus, Nereid, and Pluto's five moons. Smaller and distant irregular populations are omitted. Ceres, Sedna and Farfarout use approximate two-body elements; Farfarout's orbit remains uncertain. Nearby stellar systems use proportional J2000 3D positions. Their straight trails show approximately 2,000 years of past motion in a Galactic frame; faint brown-dwarf velocities are less certain. Brighter orbit sections are nearer. Orbit tails remain solid at each body and fade at their far ends; faster bodies have longer tails. Right-drag tilts and rotates the view. In free navigation, wheel or pinch zoom also advances the world-space rotation point along the viewing direction; panning moves it sideways. Zooming inward over a visible body temporarily anchors rotation to that body's true 3D position without selecting it; panning releases the anchor. A selected body remains the fixed rotation point until you pan away. Inner, Outer, Distant and Neighbourhood are shortcuts on one continuous kilometre-based scale; units and label detail change automatically.

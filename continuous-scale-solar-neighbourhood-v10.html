About this visualization

Live UTC positions use approximate J2000 ecliptic ephemerides from JPL orbital elements.

Natural satellites use approximate parent-relative mean-element models: the Moon, both Martian moons, and the regular moons of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Triton, Nereid, and Pluto's five moons are retained; other distant irregular populations are omitted. Haumea's two moons, Makemake's provisional MK2, and Eris's Dysnomia use simplified local models. Ceres, Haumea, Makemake, Eris, Vesta, Pallas, 16 Psyche, 1036 Ganymed, Sedna and Farfarout use approximate two-body elements; Farfarout's orbit remains uncertain. Nearby stellar systems use proportional J2000 3D positions. Their straight trails show approximately 2,000 years of past motion in a Galactic frame; faint brown-dwarf velocities are less certain.

Earth–Moon and Pluto–Charon use mass-weighted barycentric relationships. Their system barycentres own the external solar trajectories, while each physical member owns its local loop. Selection emphasis passes smoothly between the system and local trajectories as their useful display scale changes. Other moon systems remain parent-relative approximations.

Selecting the Sun reveals a cached reconstruction of its irregular motion around the Solar System barycentre from May 15, 1982 to the present. The curve is sampled monthly from the same approximate planetary elements as the live scene; it is an orientation aid rather than a precision historical ephemeris.

Brighter orbit sections are nearer. Trail and label strength reflect object importance, zoom relevance and focus; hover temporarily emphasizes only its target and reveals its complete orbit, while selection establishes persistent focus, shows the selected object's full orbit, and dims unrelated objects. Orbit tails remain solid at each body and fade at their far ends. Major planets retain speed-scaled long trails, while dwarf planets, asteroids and moons use shorter class-scaled arcs.

Right-drag tilts and rotates the view. In free navigation, wheel or pinch zoom also advances the world-space rotation point along the viewing direction; panning moves it sideways. Zooming inward over a visible body temporarily anchors rotation to that body's true 3D position without selecting it; panning releases the anchor. A selected body remains the fixed rotation point until you pan away.

Virtual points include barycentres and approximate instantaneous Sun–Earth and Earth–Moon L1–L5 locations. Lagrange points use the circular restricted three-body approximation and are intended for orientation, not navigation.

When the Solar System barycentre is outside the viewport, an edge arrow points toward it. Select once for information; double-click on desktop or tap the selected target again on mobile to centre and fit relevant detail. Selecting a Lagrange point reveals only its geometric construction line or triangle; these guides are not physical trajectories and never move the camera.

The five officially recognized dwarf planets are included. Inner, Outer, Distant and Neighbourhood are shortcuts on one continuous kilometre-based scale; units and label detail change automatically.
